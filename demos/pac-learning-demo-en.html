What are you seeing?

A small experiment with a finite hypothesis class in the realizable, consistent-learner setting.

theoretical + simulation

① Region area is error rate

The distribution on the disk is uniform. The colored region Eᵢ for hᵢ occupies fraction pᵢ of the disk, which is its true error rate err(hᵢ).

② A hit eliminates

The true label is fixed at 0. When a sample lands in Eᵢ, hᵢ predicts incorrectly, so it is inconsistent with the training sample and is removed from the version space Vₙ.

③ Large regions disappear sooner

A hypothesis with error rate p has probability (1−p)ⁿ of avoiding a hit after n draws. The larger p is, the faster its survival probability falls.

Finite-class PAC boundmPAC = ⌈ ln(K / δ) / −ln(1 − ε) ⌉
Failure upper bound for this runP(∃ ε-bad hypothesis still alive) ≤ min(1, K · (1 − ε)ⁿ)

Important: n ≥ mPAC means that, over repeated fresh experiments, with probability at least 1−δ, every hypothesis still consistent with the samples has err(h) < ε. It does not force the last bad hypothesis to disappear in one run; keep sampling if one remains.

Construction: error regions are pairwise disjoint, occupy 72% of the disk, and are ordered from largest to smallest. Each sample can hit at most one error region, making the rule-out process easy to see.
