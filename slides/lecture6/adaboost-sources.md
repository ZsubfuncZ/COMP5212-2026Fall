# AdaBoost primary-source ledger

Retrieved 2026-09-23; algorithm and conditional training-error bound rechecked against Schapire (2003), Figure 1 and Section 3, on 2026-09-24. No external figures are reproduced. Diagrams and numerical examples are original computed teaching illustrations.

1. Yoav Freund and Robert E. Schapire (1997), *A Decision-Theoretic Generalization of On-Line Learning and an Application to Boosting*, Journal of Computer and System Sciences 55(1), 119–139. Author-hosted PDF: https://www.schapire.net/papers/FreundSc95.pdf . The manuscript identifies the 1997 journal publication on its first page. Section 4 supports the adaptive reweighting algorithm and binary training-error guarantee.
2. Robert E. Schapire (2003), *The Boosting Approach to Machine Learning: An Overview*. PDF: https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/spr08/cos424/readings/Schapire2003.pdf . Figure 1 and Sections 2–3 give the ±1 convention, exponential weight update, alpha=0.5 log((1−epsilon)/epsilon), and product bound. Section 6 gives the half-log-odds population optimum and relation to logistic loss. This is an author-written research overview, not a third-party tutorial.
3. Jerome Friedman, Trevor Hastie, and Robert Tibshirani (2000), *Additive Logistic Regression: A Statistical View of Boosting*, Annals of Statistics 28(2), 337–407. Published record: https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1016218223 . Accessible author preprint (23 July 1998): https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~bhiksha/courses/mlsp.fall2010/class7/gentleboost.pdf . Sections 2–3 derive forward stagewise additive modeling and the exponential-risk population minimizer. The manuscript's Algorithm 1 uses a doubled vote convention; this deck consistently uses alpha equal to half the log error odds and exp(−alpha*y*h) weights.

The presentation's finite-sample derivation is exact greedy minimization of exponential loss over binary weak learners, assuming a positive edge. It does not identify exponential loss with Bernoulli negative log likelihood. The population probability connection is explicitly qualified as unrestricted and does not assert calibration of a finite fitted ensemble.

Numerical evidence: `examples/adaboost_example.py` exhaustively searches thresholds 1.5,...,7.5 and both orientations on x=1,...,8 with labels (−1,−1,+1,+1,−1,−1,+1,+1). Ties choose the smallest threshold, then the positive orientation. Three rounds produce errors 1/4, 1/6, 1/5 and vote weights approximately 0.5493, 0.8047, 0.6931. Exact example weights, scores, normalizers, and checks are exported to JSON and CSV.

Revision note: the new introductory frame separates observation weights D from vote coefficients alpha, directly following the algorithm in Schapire (2003). The training guarantee is conditional on a positive edge on every encountered reweighted distribution; the stronger standard weak-learning assumption over all distributions is explained in notes. No guarantee is inferred from weak performance on the original uniform distribution alone. No new empirical claims or external figures were added.
